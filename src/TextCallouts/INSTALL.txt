TextCallouts 1.0.0
==================

LSPDFR callouts that need nothing but LSPDFR and RAGE Plugin Hook.


WHAT IT IS
----------

Thirteen callouts in the style of the big callout packs, written from scratch, with no dependency on
StopThePed, Ultimate Backup, CompuLite, Callout Interface, ExternalPoliceComputer, Common Data
Framework or RAGENativeUI. You can run it on a bare LSPDFR install, and it will not conflict with
anything, because it never reaches into another plugin.

That is not a claim - it is the assembly's reference table:

    LSPD First Response, RagePluginHook, mscorlib, System, System.Core, System.Drawing, Microsoft.CSharp

Nothing else. Every dependency the big packs have is one this pack does not.

Note: it is *inspired by* packs like 686 Callouts, not a copy of it. All thirteen callouts, their names
and their dialogue are original, and none of 686 Callouts' files, text or ideas-in-code are used.
Keep 686 installed alongside if you like - they do not overlap.


THE CALLOUTS
------------

    Occupied Stolen Vehicle   A stolen car with a driver in it. Try the stop; he runs, and LSPDFR's
                              own pursuit AI drives him.
    Reckless Driver           Someone driving badly. Nothing criminal until they see you and run.
    Domestic Disturbance      Two people, half of them just arguing and half of them not. One may
                              square up to you.
    Shoplifting In Progress   On foot, and usually running: a foot pursuit you have to win.
    Armed Robbery             Armed from the start. Ask for backup before you close in.
    Two-Car Collision         Nobody to arrest. One injured driver, and an ambulance to call - the
                              one callout in the pack about helping rather than catching.
    Suspicious Person         May be nothing at all, may be a burglary suspect who runs. The record
                              comes back over the radio when you get close.
    Officer Needs Assistance  Shots fired, two armed suspects, backup rolling. The hard one.
    Barricaded Suspect        Held up inside, armed, refusing to come out. Rush him and it becomes a
                              shooting; give him time and he may walk out with his hands up.
    Stolen Vehicle (Foot)     The stolen car is parked with the driver still in it - and he bails
                              rather than drives.
    Public Disturbance        A fight in the street: two going at each other, a third watching. They
                              usually scatter when you arrive, and sometimes they do not.
    Bicycle Theft             A bike taken from outside a shop and a suspect who rides, not runs.
    Weapons Call              Armed but not yet hostile. Arrive fast and he panics; hold back and he
                              may put it down.


YOUR OWN CALLOUTS
-----------------

Drop an .xml file in Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\Custom\ and go on duty: it is offered like any
other callout. The folder appears on first run with a README.txt listing every field and a worked
example to copy, so there is nothing to look up online.

A file the pack cannot make sense of is named in Plugins\LSPDFR\textcallouts.log with the reason -
never skipped in silence.


INSTALL
-------

1. Copy TextCallouts.dll into:

       <your Grand Theft Auto V folder>\Plugins\LSPDFR\

2. Start the game with RagePluginHook.exe, load story mode.

3. GO ON DUTY: press E at a police station.

   The callouts are registered at that moment - LSPDFR loads every plugin in Plugins\LSPDFR when you
   go on duty, and not before. Until then nothing happens, which is normal.

4. Dispatch will start offering them the way it offers any callout. Accept with LSPDFR's own control,
   or with TextDispatch's /accept if you have it.


WITH TEXTDISPATCH
-----------------

If TextDispatch is installed, this pack finds it and mirrors every line it says into the chat box -
so the ambulance request, the pursuit call-in and the suspect descriptions turn up as text rather
than as notifications. That link is done with reflection at runtime: no dependency in either
direction, and if TextDispatch is not there, nothing breaks - the lines simply appear as
notifications instead.

TextDispatch also picks the callouts up automatically, because it reads each callout out of the
assemblies it finds: /calls lists all thirteen, and /callout <name> starts one on demand, which is the
quickest way to see one without waiting for dispatch.


CHECKING IT
-----------

Log: Plugins\LSPDFR\textcallouts.log - what was registered, what was accepted, what each callout
said, and any native that did not resolve.

In the F4 console:

    tcstatus     how many callouts are in the pack, whether they are registered, and the log path
    tccallouts   list them by their dispatch names


REQUIREMENTS
------------

Grand Theft Auto V (Legacy), RAGE Plugin Hook and LSPDFR 0.4.9 or newer. .NET Framework 4.8.
Nothing else - no RAGENativeUI, no interaction framework, no external callout libraries. (The only
assemblies this pack references are LSPD First Response, RagePluginHook and parts of .NET itself.)

RAGE Plugin Hook and LSPDFR are not included and never will be: both are separate works under their
own licences.
