TextCallouts %VERSION%
=====================

LSPDFR callouts that need nothing but LSPDFR and RAGE Plugin Hook.


WHAT IT IS
----------

Three hundred and forty-four callouts in the style of the big callout packs, written from scratch, with no dependency on
StopThePed, Ultimate Backup, CompuLite, Callout Interface, ExternalPoliceComputer, Common Data
Framework or RAGENativeUI. You can run it on a bare LSPDFR install, and it will not conflict with
anything, because it never reaches into another plugin.

That is not a claim - it is the assembly's reference table:

    LSPD First Response, RagePluginHook, mscorlib, System, System.Core, System.Drawing, Microsoft.CSharp

Nothing else. Every dependency the big packs have is one this pack does not.

Five of the eighteen are medical, not police work: a cardiac arrest, an overdose, a collision with
somebody trapped in the car, a welfare check and a vehicle fire. They are offered only while you are on
duty as an emergency-medical agency - LSFD, or any agency whose name says ems, medic, paramedic,
ambulance or fire - so a police patrol never sees them. Go on duty as LSFD to get them.
Note: it is *inspired by* packs like 686 Callouts, not a copy of it. All eighteen callouts, their names
and their dialogue are original, and none of 686 Callouts' files, text or ideas-in-code are used.
Keep 686 installed alongside if you like - they do not overlap.


### The library, and which duty gets what

Each callout says which duty it belongs to in its `<For>` element, so the work matches the agency you
went on duty as:

A callout offered to several duties is counted once for each of them, so this adds up to more than the
pack has:

| Duty | Callouts | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **LSPD** | 144 | city work: robberies, drugs, shots fired, burglary, assaults, disturbances |
| **Sheriff** (lssd) | 60 | county work: ranch trespass, poaching, livestock theft, rural burglary, the desert, Sandy Shores |
| **EMS** (lsfd) | 59 | patients: falls, overdoses, seizures, chest pain, cyclists, exposure |
| **Highway Patrol** (sahp) | 40 | traffic: racing, wrong-way drivers, hit and run, insecure loads, road rage |
| **Fire** (lsfd_fire) | 33 | fires: refuse, kitchens, grass, vehicles, a commercial bin fire |
| **Park Rangers** (sapr) | 28 | the parks: illegal fires, poaching, lost walkers, injured wildlife, off-roading |
| **Prison** (saspa) | 16 | Bolingbroke: escapes, contraband drops, transport incidents, assaults on staff |
| **Any agency** | 2 | an officer needs assistance, an assault on an officer - offered to whoever is on duty |

Three hundred and twenty-six of those live in `Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\Library` as XML recipes - the same format a
player's own callouts use, so you can read them, copy them and edit them. The other eighteen are written
in C# because their behaviour is particular (the medical ones, and the police ones that need a scene the
recipes cannot describe). A recipe names a duty with `police`, one agency, or several: `<For>lspd,sheriff,sahp</For>`
is offered to all three. An agency the pack has never heard of gets the police work, because a patrol
with no calls at all is a worse failure than a misfiled one.

WHAT THEY SAY
------------

**Every callout in the pack has a script, and the people in it talk.**

Walk up to the suspect in an armed robbery and type at them, and they answer in character - out of that
callout's own lines, in order, with no language model involved:

    I have not touched the till. Ask them, I have not touched it.
    The gun is not real. Look at it, it is not real.
    I came in for cigarettes. That is the whole of it.

Three or four lines each, nine hundred and eighty-one of them across the three hundred and twenty-six
recipes. Somebody who has said their piece repeats their last line rather than going mute. In a medical
callout it is the patient who answers, not the person stood next to them - so asking a woman with chest
pain how it started gets you the answer you want before you move her.

That needs TextDispatch, which asks for a callout's lines before it asks its own script or a model - see
WITH TEXTDISPATCH below. Without it the lines are simply never asked for, and a callout behaves exactly as
it did before they existed.

A callout can also say what happens after you have been at the scene a while - backup twenty seconds in, a
suspect's nerve breaking at fifty, the fire catching once they have had a chance to talk. Four callouts use
that to start with: a standoff, a bank siege, a gang fight, and a patient who wants to talk to you before
you treat her.

THE CALLOUTS
------------

    Occupied Stolen Vehicle   A stolen car with a driver in it. Try the stop; he runs, and LSPDFR's
                              own pursuit AI drives him.
    Reckless Driver           Someone driving badly. Nothing criminal until they see you and run.
    Domestic Disturbance      Two people, half of them just arguing and half of them not. One may
                              square up to you.
    Shoplifting In Progress   On foot, and usually running: a foot pursuit you have to win.
    Armed Robbery             Armed from the start. Ask for backup before you close in.
    Two-Car Collision         Nobody to arrest. One injured driver, and an ambulance to call - the
                              one callout in the pack about helping rather than catching.
    Suspicious Person         May be nothing at all, may be a burglary suspect who runs. The record
                              comes back over the radio when you get close.
    Officer Needs Assistance  Shots fired, two armed suspects, backup rolling. The hard one.
    Barricaded Suspect        Held up inside, armed, refusing to come out. Rush him and it becomes a
                              shooting; give him time and he may walk out with his hands up.
    Stolen Vehicle (Foot)     The stolen car is parked with the driver still in it - and he bails
                              rather than drives.
    Public Disturbance        A fight in the street: two going at each other, a third watching. They
                              usually scatter when you arrive, and sometimes they do not.
    Bicycle Theft             A bike taken from outside a shop and a suspect who rides, not runs.
    Weapons Call              Armed but not yet hostile. Arrive fast and he panics; hold back and he
                              may put it down.


YOUR OWN CALLOUTS
-----------------

Drop an .xml file in Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\Custom\ and go on duty: it is offered like any
other callout. The folder appears on first run with a README.txt listing every field and a worked
example to copy, so there is nothing to look up online.

A file the pack cannot make sense of is named in Plugins\LSPDFR\textcallouts.log with the reason -
never skipped in silence.


INSTALL
-------

1. Copy TextCallouts.dll into:

       <your Grand Theft Auto V folder>\Plugins\LSPDFR\

2. Start the game with RagePluginHook.exe, load story mode.

3. GO ON DUTY: press E at a police station.

   The callouts are registered at that moment - LSPDFR loads every plugin in Plugins\LSPDFR when you
   go on duty, and not before. Until then nothing happens, which is normal.

4. Dispatch will start offering them the way it offers any callout. Accept with LSPDFR's own control,
   or with TextDispatch's /accept if you have it.


WITH TEXTDISPATCH
-----------------

If TextDispatch is installed, this pack finds it and mirrors every line it says into the chat box -
so the ambulance request, the pursuit call-in and the suspect descriptions turn up as text rather
than as notifications. That link is done with reflection at runtime: no dependency in either
direction, and if TextDispatch is not there, nothing breaks - the lines simply appear as
notifications instead.

TextDispatch asks this pack what a callout's people should say when they are spoken to, and it asks
**before** its own scripted replies and before any language model. That is what the scripts above are for:
press the box key (left arrow), type at whoever is nearest, and they answer out of the callout's own lines.
The pack's log records it as it hands them over:

    script for lspd-armed-robbery-off-licence: 3 line(s), to be spoken by the suspect when the player
    talks to them

TextDispatch also picks the callouts up automatically, because it reads each callout out of the
assemblies it finds: /calls lists all eighteen, and /callout <name> starts one on demand, which is the
quickest way to see one without waiting for dispatch.


CHECKING IT
-----------

Log: Plugins\LSPDFR\textcallouts.log - what was registered, what was accepted, what each callout
said, and any native that did not resolve.

In the F4 console:

    tcstatus     how many callouts are in the pack, whether they are registered, and the log path
    tccallouts   list them by their dispatch names


REQUIREMENTS
------------

Grand Theft Auto V (Legacy), RAGE Plugin Hook and LSPDFR 0.4.9 or newer. .NET Framework 4.8.
Nothing else - no RAGENativeUI, no interaction framework, no external callout libraries. (The only
assemblies this pack references are LSPD First Response, RagePluginHook and parts of .NET itself.)

RAGE Plugin Hook and LSPDFR are not included and never will be: both are separate works under their
own licences.
