bundle %BUNDLE_VERSION%
=======================

LSPDFR - and DriverJobs - in text. Three plugins that are meant to be installed together:

    TextDispatch %DISPATCH_VERSION%    the police side: dispatch, the people in the callouts,
                                       traffic stops, records, all through a chat box
    TextCallouts %CALLOUTS_VERSION%    four hundred and sixty-two callouts, written from scratch, needing nothing but
                                       LSPDFR and RAGE Plugin Hook
    TextJobs %TEXTJOBS_VERSION%        the civilian side: DriverJobs V's jobs in a box of their own,
                                       needing neither LSPDFR nor RAGE Plugin Hook

All three DLLs are in this zip. Neither RAGE Plugin Hook nor LSPDFR is - they are separate works under
their own licences, and you need your own working install of both. TextJobs does not need either of
them, but it does need ScriptHookV and ScriptHookVDotNet, which DriverJobs V needs anyway.


INSTALL
-------

1. Copy these TWO DLLs into:

       <your Grand Theft Auto V folder>\Plugins\LSPDFR\
           TextDispatch.dll
           TextCallouts.dll

   Create the folder if it is not there. Plugins\LSPDFR - not Plugins, and not the game's lspdfr\
   data folder. Only that folder is loaded by LSPDFR into its own AppDomain, which is what lets a
   callout pack see the callout API at all.

2. Copy this THIRD DLL into:

       <your Grand Theft Auto V folder>\scripts\
           TextJobs.dll

   That is the folder DriverJobs V is in. Not Plugins\LSPDFR: LSPDFR and ScriptHookVDotNet read
   different folders, and a plugin in the wrong one is never loaded at all.

3. Copy the FOLDER of callout recipes into:

       <your Grand Theft Auto V folder>\Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\

   ...so the files end up in Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\Library\. That is TextCallouts' own library:
   four hundred and forty-four of its four hundred and sixty-two callouts, written as XML recipes and sorted by the duty they
   belong to - LSPD, Sheriff, Highway Patrol, Rangers, the prison, EMS and fire. Skip this step and the
   eighteen callouts written in C# still work; you just lose the other four hundred and forty-four, and the log says so.

4. Start the game with RagePluginHook.exe, not the normal launcher.

5. Load story mode, then GO ON DUTY: press E at a police station.

   This is the step people miss, for the two police plugins. LSPDFR loads everything in
   Plugins\LSPDFR at that moment and not before it - no chat box, no callouts, nothing, however long
   you wait in story mode. TextCallouts registers its callouts at the same moment and says so
   on screen.

   TextJobs does not wait for any of that: it loads with the game, and a ticker says it did.

6. The keys, and there are two because there are two boxes:

       LEFT ARROW    the police box (TextDispatch)     /key Left, or /key F6
       F9            the jobs box   (TextJobs)         /key F9

   Neither is T, which other mods claim on a lot of installs. /chatter quiet|brief|full decides how
   much of the dispatcher's radio traffic reaches the police box.


WHAT THEY DO TOGETHER
---------------------

Installed side by side, they are one experience:

  * the callouts arrive as radio traffic in the chat box, and you accept or decline by typing - four hundred and sixty-two of them, sorted by what you went on duty as

    Five of those eighteen are not police work: a cardiac arrest, an overdose, a collision with
    somebody trapped in the car, a welfare check and a vehicle fire. They are offered only while you
    are on duty as an emergency-medical agency - LSFD, or any agency whose name says ems, medic,
    paramedic, ambulance or fire - and stay out of the way on a police patrol. Going on duty as LSFD
    is how you get them.
  * every line a callout says - the ambulance request, the pursuit call-in, the suspect description -
    is mirrored into the chat box instead of a notification box
  * /calls lists all eighteen alongside LSPDFR's own callouts and any other pack's, and /callout <name>
    starts one on demand, which is the quickest way to see one
  * the people the callouts send you to are drawn at random each time, and they are men and women:
    sixty-three ped models in pools of two to twenty, chosen to suit the callout - so three suspects are
    three different people and the person in the armed robbery is rarely the same one twice. Two calls
    are deliberately one sex because their own name says so. Vehicles are pooled the same way, except
    where the vehicle *is* the callout. Every recipe carries a fallback model as well as a pool, checked
    against your game before anything spawns, so a pool that is wrong for an install cannot leave a
    scene with nobody in it
  * the agencies that had nothing have work of their own: the FIB, the IAA, the DOA, the NOOSE and North
    Yankton were getting fifteen calls each and none of them federal, because no recipe said "any police
    agency". They now have warrants, watches, informant meets, raids and sieges - 21 to 35 calls each
  * people in the callouts answer in text when you talk to them - and every callout has its own script,
    one thousand three hundred and thirty-five lines across the four hundred and forty-four recipes. Type at the suspect
    in an armed robbery and he denies it in character, out of that callout's own lines, with no model
    running at all: TextDispatch asks the callout what its people say *before* it asks its own script or
    Ollama. In a medical callout it is the patient who answers, so asking how it started gets you the
    answer before you move her
  * your own callouts - written as XML files in Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\Custom\ - are offered
    exactly like the built-in eighteen, no compiling involved
  * and the civilian side: /jobs lists every job DriverJobs V has, with what each pays, and
    /job <name or number> describes one and puts a blip and a sat-nav route on where it starts

Both boxes hide what you type from every other plugin and script while they are open, so typing
"10-97" or "San Andreas Freight" cannot open somebody else's menu.

None of that is a hard dependency. Each plugin works alone: TextDispatch runs with no callout pack,
TextCallouts runs with no text interface - its lines simply arrive as normal notifications instead -
and TextJobs runs with no LSPDFR at all. The link between TextDispatch and TextCallouts is made at
runtime by reflection, so installing one without the other breaks nothing.


CHECKING IT
-----------

Rage Plugin Hook's log (RagePluginHook.log, and dated copies in Logs\) shows what loaded. Look for
"Creating plugin: TextDispatch.Main" and "Creating plugin: TextCallouts.Main" - they only appear
after you go on duty.

In textcallouts.log, after going on duty, two lines say whether the callout library is in the game:

    callouts from files: 326 loaded, 0 skipped (326 from the library, 0 of your own)
    registered for this duty: 18 of 18 built-in callouts, 326 of 326 from the library, 0 of 0 of your own

If the second says "0 of 0 from the library", the Library folder is not in
Plugins\LSPDFR\TextCallouts\ - copy it in from this download and go on duty again. The plugin and its
326 recipes ship together, and they are meant to be installed together.

    Plugins\LSPDFR\textdispatch.log    what loaded, and what each plugin in the folder is missing
    Plugins\LSPDFR\textcallouts.log    which callouts registered, what each one said, which native ran
    scripts\TextJobs.log               the job list it read, and the whole list every time /jobs runs
    ScriptHookVDotNet.log              "Loaded 2 script(s)" when DriverJobs and TextJobs are both in

In the police box:  /plugins  /calls  /help
In the jobs box:    /jobs  /job <name>  /help
In the F4 console:  tdstatus, tdplugins, tcstatus, tccallouts


REQUIREMENTS
------------

For the two police plugins: Grand Theft Auto V (Legacy or Enhanced), RAGE Plugin Hook, LSPDFR 0.4.9 or
newer, and .NET Framework 4.8.

For TextJobs: the same game, ScriptHookV and ScriptHookVDotNet - the ones DriverJobs V already needs -
and DriverJobs V itself if you want the job list to have anything in it. Not LSPDFR, and not RAGE
Plugin Hook.

Optional, for TextDispatch's conversation: a local model on your own machine - Ollama on
localhost:11434, or LM Studio. TextDispatch works without one, on a built-in script.

Nothing else is required. In particular TextCallouts needs no RAGENativeUI, no StopThePed, no
Ultimate Backup, no CompuLite, no Callout Interface, no External PoliceComputer, no Common Data
Framework - the complete list of assemblies it references is LSPD First Response, RagePluginHook and
the .NET framework.
